Why Cooking Gas Is the Perfect Side Hustle

Think about it. Every Nigerian home that cooks with gas needs a refill at least once a month. Some homes refill twice a month. That is a recurring customer — the holy grail of business.

Gas is not a luxury anymore. It is a necessity. Kerosene is expensive and dirty. Firewood is stressful and unhealthy. Electricity? Unreliable. Nigerians are switching to gas every single day, and they need someone trustworthy to sell it to them.

That someone can be you.

Can You Really Start From Home?

Yes. You do not need a fancy shop on the main road. You do not need a big signboard. You do not even need to rent a separate space.

Here is what you actually need:

  • A small corner in your compound, veranda, or backyard
  • Good ventilation — gas is heavier than air, so it sinks. You need open space where gas can escape if there is ever a leak
  • A concrete or tiled floor — no bare sand or grass
  • Distance from open flames — keep your setup away from your kitchen fire, generator, or candles
  • A small lockable storage for your cylinders when you are not around

I know a woman in Warri who runs her gas business from her veranda. Her husband built a small metal cage to hold her cylinders. She sells to her neighbors, her church members, and people who find her through WhatsApp. She makes ₦80,000 to ₦120,000 profit every month. From her veranda.

The Essential Equipment Every Gas Retailer Needs

Let me list exactly what you need, what it does, and what it costs. These are real prices from the Nigerian market as of August 2026.

1. Gas Cylinders (Your Storage Tanks)

You need at least one large cylinder to store bulk gas. This is what you fill at the depot, then dispense into customer cylinders.

A 50kg LPG cylinder is the industry standard for small retailers. It holds enough gas to fill roughly four 12.5kg customer cylinders. A brand new, SON-certified 50kg cylinder with brass valve costs between ₦300,000 to ₦350,000 in the open market.

Pro tip: If your budget is tight, you can start with a 25kg cylinder (₦180,000 to ₦220,000) and upgrade later. I started with one 50kg cylinder. Today I have four.

2. Digital Weighing Scale

This is the most important tool in your business. Customers will not trust you if you guess the weight. A fake scale or no scale will kill your reputation before you even start.

You need a digital platform scale that can weigh up to 100kg. It should have:

  • LCD display showing weight, unit price, and total price
  • Price computing function (weight × price per kg = total)
  • Rechargeable battery (for when NEPA takes light)
  • Heavy-duty steel platform
  • Overload protection

A good 100kg digital platform scale costs between ₦150,000 to ₦200,000. Do not buy the cheap ₦30,000 scales from the roadside. They break in two months and your customers will notice the inconsistency.

3. Dispensing Hose & Brass Nozzle

You need a high-pressure LPG hose with a brass nozzle for transferring gas from your storage cylinder to customer cylinders. Budget ₦15,000 to ₦25,000 for a quality set that will not leak or crack.

4. Fire Extinguisher (Non-Negotiable)

Gas is flammable. One mistake and everything burns. You need at least one standard 6kg ABC dry powder fire extinguisher. Cost: ₦12,000 to ₦18,000. Check the pressure gauge monthly. It is not decoration. It is insurance.

5. Safety Signage

Print or paint these signs and place them where customers can see:

  • "NO SMOKING — HIGHLY FLAMMABLE"
  • "NO OPEN FLAME"
  • "SON CERTIFIED CYLINDERS ONLY"

Budget ₦5,000 to ₦10,000 for signage.

6. Safety Apron & Gloves

A simple thick apron and a pair of industrial gloves protect you during refills. Budget ₦5,000 to ₦8,000.

7. Padlock & Chain

Your cylinders are valuable. Lock them up when you close for the day. Budget ₦3,000 to ₦5,000.

The Profit Math: How Much Can You Really Make?

Here is what matters. Let me show you the numbers.

Depot price (August 2026): ~₦1,090 per kg

Your selling price: ₦1,200 to ₦1,600 per kg (depending on location)

Your profit per kg: ₦110 to ₦510

Example — Starter Package (25kg cylinder, home-based):

You buy 50kg of gas at the depot for ₦54,500.
You sell it at ₦1,400 per kg.
Revenue: 50kg × ₦1,400 = ₦70,000
Profit: ₦70,000 − ₦54,500 = ₦15,500

Do this 4 times a month = ₦62,000 monthly profit from one small cylinder.

As your customer base grows, you sell more. Scale to 100kg per week and you are making ₦120,000+ per month from your veranda.

Registration: What You Actually Need (And What You Can Delay)

Let me be honest with you. Not every home-based gas seller has every license on day one. But here is what you should prioritize:

Immediate (Do This First)

  • CAC Business Name Registration — ₦15,000 to ₦25,000. This gives you legitimacy. My business, OGas Ventures, is registered under CAC BN 9638951. Customers trust a registered business.
  • Local Government Trade Permit — ₦10,000 to ₦30,000 per year. Prevents harassment from local task forces.

Within 3 Months (As You Grow)

  • DPR Retail License — ₦50,000 to ₦100,000. The Department of Petroleum Resources inspects your premises for safety. Required if you want to operate at scale or partner with bigger depots.
  • Fire Service Clearance — ₦15,000 to ₦25,000. Some states require this. It is worth getting even if not mandatory.

Start with CAC and local government permit. Add DPR as your revenue grows. Do not let paperwork stop you from starting.

The Mistakes That Kill New Gas Businesses

I have watched people fail. Here is what not to do:

Mistake 1: Buying Fake or Uncertified Cylinders

Only buy SON-certified cylinders with brass valves. A fake cylinder can explode. Your life, your customers' lives, and your compound are not worth saving ₦50,000 on a substandard tank.

Mistake 2: Skipping the Scale

I have seen sellers try to run this business with no scale. They guess the weight. Customers notice. Word spreads. One underfilled cylinder and you lose five customers. Buy a proper digital scale. It pays for itself in trust.

Mistake 3: Ignoring Safety

Gas is heavier than air. If it leaks, it sinks to the ground and pools. One spark and everything burns. Keep your setup ventilated. Keep fire away. Keep a working extinguisher nearby. Teach your family — especially children — that the gas corner is off-limits.

Mistake 4: Cheating on Weight

Some sellers think underfilling is smart business. It is not. It is the fastest way to destroy your reputation. I built Mega Think Success on one promise: exact weight, every time. My customers know that when Simon fills their cylinder, they get every kilogram they paid for. That is why they come back. That is why they send their friends.

Mistake 5: Staying Invisible

In 2026, customers find sellers online. If you are only waiting for walk-ins, you are leaving money on the table. List your shop on OGas Marketplace. Share your prices on WhatsApp status. Tell your church group. Post in your community Facebook group. Visibility equals sales.
